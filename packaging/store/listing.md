## Description

Tessera arranges your windows for you. Split each monitor into zones, and every zone tiles the
windows inside it automatically: side by side, stacked with tabs, or one window filling the
zone. Or use a single zone per monitor for pure auto-tiling.

ZONES AND TILING TOGETHER
• Each monitor and each virtual desktop has its own layout: columns, rows, grids, nested splits
  or freely placed rectangles.
• New windows split the focused one, join a stack of tabs, or line up as columns or rows,
  depending on the zone's mode.
• Windows land back in their zones after a restart.

KEYBOARD FIRST
• Move focus and windows with Win+Alt+H/J/K/L or the arrow keys, across zones and monitors.
• Send a window to zone 1–9, cycle layouts, toggle floating, monocle or stack mode.
• Window hints: press Win+Alt+W and type the letters shown on a window to jump to it, swap it
  or pull it into a stack.
• A keyboard shortcuts window lists every action, shows conflicts with Windows shortcuts and
  lets you record new shortcuts.

MOUSE TOO
• Hold Shift while dragging to see the zones and drop a window exactly where you want it.
• Drag the edge between two tiles to resize them; click tabs to switch stacked windows.

MORE
• Zone editor: click to split zones on top of your actual monitor.
• Scratchpads: summon a terminal or notes window with one shortcut and hide it again.
• Scenes: save the arrangement of all monitors and restore it automatically when you dock or
  undock your laptop.
• Window rules: ignore, float or place programs by name, class or title.
• Plain-text configuration (TOML) that reloads as soon as you save it.
• Command line (tesserac) for scripting.
• Switch and move windows between virtual desktops.

PRIVATE AND LIGHTWEIGHT
Tessera runs in the system tray, needs no administrator rights, collects no data and never
connects to the internet.

OPEN SOURCE
Tessera is free and open source under the MIT license:
https://github.com/Padishar24/tessera
It was developed with AI assistance (Anthropic's Claude), directed and tested by a human.
